# Licensing decision — unresolved

The repository LICENSE remains unchanged. Website copy now refers to the relevant release license rather than advertising MIT as a permanent commitment.

- Apache-2.0 permits commercial reuse, including proprietary derivatives subject to its conditions. It will not prevent competing paid products.
- GPL permits selling software. Its copyleft obligations apply to covered distribution; ordinary hosted operation does not itself create a general source-sharing obligation.
- AGPL also permits commercial use. Its network clause requires a modified covered program to offer corresponding source to remote users. This can discourage closed-source hosted forks, but cannot prevent paid hosting competitors.
- A prohibition on commercial use or competing hosting requires a carefully chosen source-available/commercial restriction. Such a restriction is not open source under the OSI definition.

For the founder's stated aim, choose between genuine open source with reciprocity (evaluate AGPL) and source availability with commercial restrictions. Review third-party/contributor rights before relicensing. A new license does not revoke rights already granted for existing MIT releases.
